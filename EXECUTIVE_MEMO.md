# Business Affairs memo — value the rights being surrendered

**Audience:** Television Business Affairs, Distribution and FP&A  
**Status:** Independent fictional case, prepared September 29, 2026  
**Decision:** Prefer Offer C, subject to confirming rights and the displaced-revenue assumptions.

The illustrative package contains twelve completed series and covers US English-language rights over three years. The alternatives are mutually exclusive. A full-term exclusive deal offers $42m, a non-exclusive deal offers $24m, and a shorter exclusive window offers $31m.

After foregone catalog revenue, modeled participation, timing and upfront delivery costs, incremental NPVs are **$9.68m, $11.40m and $12.93m**, respectively. The $31m offer is therefore preferable under the baseline assumptions. It also provides $15.30m of incremental cash in the first year, compared with $6.60m for the full-term exclusive alternative.

The no-deal fee floor for Offer A is $30.18m. Quoting that as an acceptable negotiating threshold would ignore the other offers: **A needs $45.97m** with the modeled payment schedule to match C. Offer B needs $25.92m to match C. At its current $31m price, C has approximately $1.82m of headline-fee cushion above the $29.18m threshold that matches B.

## Proposed negotiating position

| Item | Position supported by this model | Confirm before commitment |
|---|---|---|
| Preferred structure | Short exclusive window | Exact media, territory, language, start/end dates and holdbacks |
| Fee concessions | Evaluate against best-alternative threshold | Other offers remain executable and comparable |
| Payment schedule | Preserve early receipts | Acceptance, invoicing, creditworthiness and enforceability |
| Catalog opportunity | Retain later monetization rights | Existing obligations and realistic demand after exclusivity |

The recommendation is most exposed to the assumed no-deal catalog revenue and how much of it survives each structure. The accompanying sensitivity file makes those assumptions visible across three revenue levels and discount rates. These are scenario ranges, not statistical confidence intervals.

FP&A should maintain the comparison while Business Affairs verifies legal rights and Distribution validates market demand. Accounting separately determines revenue recognition; cash receipt dates are not a substitute for that assessment. Incremental NPV is a decision aid, not an accounting profit forecast or a guaranteed realized benefit.
